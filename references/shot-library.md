# Shot and styling library

## Xiaohongshu visual grammar

Use at least three of these cues in each image:

- environment occupies 60–75%;
- subject is off-center or partially cropped;
- one foreground obstruction or reflection layer;
- shallow but believable depth of field;
- camera feels handheld or observed, not perfectly architectural;
- one dominant subject and one supporting subject;
- neutral highlights, restrained saturation, visible shadow detail;
- incomplete edges: chair, curtain, cup rim, shoulder, or door frame enters naturally.

Do not simulate “网感” with excessive grain, orange shadows, crushed blacks, fake lens flare, or random clutter.

## Hotel scene families

| Family | Useful variants |
|---|---|
| Establishing | window-led cover, doorway observation, bed-to-window layer, title-safe negative space |
| Lifestyle | static window seat, robe near window, bed-edge pause, mirror-adjacent partial figure |
| Detail | coffee and magazine, curtain hand, bedding geometry, glass reflection, chair textile |
| Quiet | no-person morning light, rainy window, blue-hour room, curtain silhouette |

## Restaurant and dining families

| Family | Useful variants |
|---|---|
| Establishing | long table with window, doorway/foreground chair, asymmetrical room overview |
| Table | rich afternoon tea, welcome drinks, plated dishes, glassware/linen detail |
| People | seated soft-focus figure, over-shoulder conversation, static edge crop, hand holding cup |
| Quiet | prepared table before guests, reflections, curtain/seat detail, bright no-person window scene |

## Person treatments

Default order for naturalness:

1. no person;
2. static soft-focus shoulder/back/profile at an edge;
3. seated hand-to-cup interaction;
4. over-shoulder conversation with the second person mostly hidden;
5. clear portrait only when explicitly requested.

Hard rules:

- one visible face unless a social scene explicitly needs two;
- no face cut by a window mullion;
- no walking or motion blur unless explicitly requested;
- keep hands few and readable; hide a hand if it is not needed;
- avoid direct camera gaze, central posing, and full-body catalog posture by default.

## Afternoon-tea minimum

A table is not complete when it contains only one cup and one pastry. For a two-person afternoon-tea scene, include a coherent baseline:

- one three-tier stand or three height-varied serving plates;
- one teapot;
- two cup-and-saucer sets;
- two dessert plates;
- five to nine small foods across at least three types: scones, fruit tarts, macarons, finger sandwiches, or small cakes;
- one low floral arrangement that does not block faces;
- two lightly folded cloth napkins;
- two water glasses or one water glass plus one small vase.

Keep negative space between groups. Use one hero vertical element, one supporting floral mass, and low horizontal service pieces. Do not duplicate every object symmetrically.

## Other dining baselines

### Drinks and welcome scene

- two or three unbranded drinks with distinct heights;
- one shared small plate of sweets or fruit;
- one floral or textile cue;
- condensation and reflections remain physically plausible.

### Dinner scene

- three to five real-looking plated dishes, not ten generic dishes;
- serving logic matches the number of diners;
- keep glassware and cutlery secondary;
- preserve actual cuisine and dish identity when the source is factual.

### Banquet or celebration

- use only when the brief asks for a group or event;
- distinguish celebration details from permanent venue architecture;
- avoid inventing balloon walls, signage, logos, or a river view.

## Bright-clear exposure target

For 清透/明亮:

- bright window or pale wall occupies about two-thirds;
- highlights are bright but retain curtain and leaf texture;
- white porcelain remains neutral, not yellow;
- greenery is light natural green, not neon;
- dark furniture retains texture and separation;
- foreground silhouettes are dark gray or soft brown, not pure black blocks.
